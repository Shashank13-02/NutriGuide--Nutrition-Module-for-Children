📋 Pediatric Nutrition Taxonomy & Variety Architecture

          
8 core dimensions derived from WHO, UNICEF, and CDC clinical standards

        

        
      


      

        

          

            
              

                Category
                Examples & Formats
                Why It Matters in Pediatric Care
              

            
            
              

                Age / development
                0–5, 6–8, 9–11, 12–23, 24–59 months
                Determines whether complementary feeding and certain textures are developmentally appropriate.
              

              

                Food groups
                Breast milk; grains/roots/tubers; pulses/nuts/seeds; dairy; flesh foods; eggs; vitamin-A-rich fruit/veg; other fruit/veg
                WHO/UNICEF’s 6–23-month dietary-diversity framework uses 8 distinct groups to prevent micronutrient deficiencies.
              

              

                Texture
                Purée, mashed, lumpy/soft pieces, safe finger food, family-food texture
                Must fit feeding skill—not age alone. Prevents choking while advancing oral motor feeding development.
              

              

                Preparation / safety
                Cooked, peeled, mashed, thinly spread, cut lengthwise/quartered
                Directly identifies possible choking risks or food-safety hazards (e.g. quartering round grapes).
              

              

                Allergen flag
                Egg, dairy, peanut/tree nut, wheat, soy, fish/shellfish
                Enables prompts to check infant history and seek clinician guidance for severe eczema or existing reactions.
              

              

                Added ingredients
                Honey (<12m), added sugar, excess salt, unpasteurized/raw foods
                Supports age-specific safety warnings (e.g. strict avoidance of infant botulism from honey).
              

              

                Meal pattern
                Meal/snack frequency; caregiver-entered daily pattern
                Complements single meal photos; a photo cannot show whole-day nutritional and energy intake.
              

              

                Confidence
                Clearly visible / uncertain / caregiver-corrected
                Prevents treating model guesses as facts; mandates caregiver confirmation before advice is generated.
